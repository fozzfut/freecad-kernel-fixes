Issue 034 - OCCT BRepOffset local self-intersection: literature pass (step STUDY, 27.09.2026)
Rule: C:/dev/cad-perf-research/_workflows/AGENT-RULES.md "LITERATURE FIRST" (IF >= 5, or specialist CAD/geometry
journals counted as full sources). Mark: [OA] open full text found, [ABS] abstract/publisher page only.

1. T. Maekawa, "An overview of offset curves and surfaces", Computer-Aided Design 31(3) 165-173, 1999,
   doi:10.1016/S0010-4485(99)00013-5. Venue: CAD (specialist, full source). [ABS]
   Idea: an offset at distance d folds locally where d exceeds the radius of curvature on the concave side
   (cusps / swallowtails), and self-intersects globally where distant parts come closer than 2d.
   Applied: the fold predictor of the study (Jacobian of P + dN changes sign) = mechanism M1; explains every
   BOP/INV result of the study except M5.
2. R.T. Farouki, "The approximation of non-degenerate offset surfaces", Computer Aided Geometric Design 3(1)
   15-43, 1986, doi:10.1016/0167-8396(86)90022-1. Venue: CAGD (specialist). [ABS]
   Idea: offset surfaces are "non-degenerate" only while |d| stays below the smallest principal radius on the
   offset side; approximation schemes are valid only there.
   Applied: the regularity condition behind the pre-check proposed for step (a).
3. J. Wallner, T. Sakkalis, T. Maekawa, H. Pottmann, G. Yu, "Self-intersections of offset curves and surfaces",
   International Journal of Shape Modeling 7(1) 1-21, 2001, doi:10.1142/S0218654301000023. [ABS]
   (IJSM: specialist shape-modeling journal, IF < 5; counted as a specialist source, flag for reviewer.)
   Idea: the maximum offset distance with no local AND no global self-intersection, boundary effects included.
   Applied: candidate for an exact (not sampled) fold bound per face in step (a).
4. J.-K. Seong, G. Elber, M.-S. Kim, "Trimming local and global self-intersections in offset curves/surfaces using
   distance maps", Computer-Aided Design 38(3) 183-193, 2006, doi:10.1016/j.cad.2005.09.002. [OA:
   http://3map.snu.ac.kr/mskim/ftp/cad06.pdf]
   Idea: a rational distance map between the base surface and its offset; the regions where the squared distance
   drops below d^2 are exactly the parts to trim (local and global).
   Applied: the study's "exact-offset distance test" (offstudy inspect) is the sampled form of this criterion; it
   is the only test here that catches M2 (valid, BOP-clean but wrong body). Route for step (b) trimming.
5. D. Pekerman, G. Elber, M.-S. Kim, "Self-intersection detection and elimination in freeform curves and
   surfaces", Computer-Aided Design 40(2) 150-159, 2008, doi:10.1016/j.cad.2007.10.004. [OA: citeseerx copy]
   Idea: binormal-line criterion + direct algebraic elimination to detect and remove local and global
   self-intersections of a freeform surface (offsets included).
   Applied: detection of the fold of a single Geom_OffsetSurface face - the case OCCT never intersects
   (BRepOffset_Inter3d::FaceInter returns for F1 == F2).
6. G.V.V. Ravi Kumar, K.G. Shastry, B.G. Prakash, "Computing non-self-intersecting offsets of NURBS surfaces",
   Computer-Aided Design 34(3) 209-228, 2002, ScienceDirect PII S0010448501000811. [ABS]
   Idea: compute NURBS offsets and remove self-intersecting parts before use in modelling.
   Applied: confirms that trimming belongs to face construction, before the B-rep assembly (step (b) placement).
7. Q. Hong, Y. Park, M.-S. Kim, G. Elber, "Trimming offset surface self-intersections around near-singular
   regions", Computers & Graphics 82 84-94, 2019 (SMI 2019), ScienceDirect PII S0097849319300779. [ABS]
   (SMI proceedings: specialist geometry venue.)
   Idea: robust trimming where the offset is near-singular (J close to 0) - exactly the threshold region
   (step NURBS 0.9 vs 1.1) measured here.
   Applied: warns that a sampled J test needs a margin near J = 0; the step (a) guard must not flip on J ~ 0.
8. (authors not verified), "Offsetting operations on non-manifold topological models", Computer-Aided Design (2009-2010),
   ScienceDirect PII S0010448509001523. [ABS, publisher page 403 - authors not re-checked]
   Idea: B-rep solid offset/thickening/shelling as offset faces + gap filling + intersection removal + sewing.
   Applied: the same pipeline shape as BRepOffset; its intersection-removal stage is what OCCT lacks for curved
   faces (OCCT does "complete intersection" only for all-planar solids).

9. "Fast Determination and Computation of Self-intersections for NURBS Surfaces", ACM Transactions on Graphics
   (2025), doi:10.1145/3727620. Venue: ACM TOG (IF >= 5). [ABS; authors not re-checked in this pass]
   Idea: fast exclusion test + computation of NURBS surface self-intersection curves.
   Applied: candidate for the fold/self-intersection test of a single offset face in step (a)/(b) when the
   sampled Jacobian is inconclusive (M5, near-singular J ~ 0).

Combination for the next steps (decision input, not yet a design):
- step (a) never silent: Farouki/Maekawa regularity bound per offset face (sampled J with a margin per 7, or the
  exact bound of 3) -> error instead of a folded face; plus API honesty (null result -> not done; NbOF == NbF must
  prove an offset shell exists) and a distance/volume sanity check derived from 4.
- step (b) trimming: 4 + 5 (distance map / algebraic self-intersection of the one face) to trim the folded region
  of each offset face before Inter3d, then the existing neighbour intersection closes the gap.

==== Stage A (guard, 27.09.2026, commit e55a6ba8) - how the sources are applied ====
Full list: C:/dev/occt8-mig/lit-offset/LITERATURE.txt (20 sources, 7 read in full). Applied in stage A:
- Farouki 1986 / Maekawa 1999 (CAD 31(3), specialist journal): offset regularity = every principal factor 1 - d*k_i > 0.
  APPLIED as the fold test (principal factors from D2, not the Jacobian product: the product misses umbilic double
  inversion, e.g. a sphere shrunk past its centre has J > 0).
- Wallner, Sakkalis, Maekawa, Pottmann, Yu 2001 (exact regularity bound): NOT applied (needs a certified curvature
  bound per patch); sampled grid + pattern search instead, with a 0.05 margin. Upgrade path: Li-Jia-Chen, ACM TOG 2025
  (certified NURBS self-intersection exclusion test).
- Seong, Elber, Kim 2006 (CAD, specialist): distance-map criterion - a correct offset lies >= |d| from the input.
  APPLIED as the post-check for plain offsets (catches M2: valid clean body of the wrong piece, vol 0.70 vs ~5579).
- Pekerman, Elber, Kim 2008; Hong, Park, Kim, Elber 2019; Park et al. 2021: trimming of the fold - stage B, not A.
- (Review offset-A, 27.09: an earlier line here cited the Parasolid offsetting chapter as the source of the policy.
  That document is quarantined under the owner IP rule and is NOT a source. The policy "an error, never a silent
  broken body" is the owner's own rule (27.09, AGENT-RULES "never silent wrong geometry"); no design element of
  stage A comes from that document - see the provenance log in README.md, section "Этап A".)
- OCCT 8.0.1 header BRepOffsetAPI_MakeOffsetShape.hxx 91-96 (documentation): the limitation the guard closes.
Better-known method check: no journal source gives a cheaper sufficient detector than curvature sampling for the
common case; certified tests (Wallner 2001, TOG 2025) are stronger and slower - kept as the upgrade.

==== Stage B (trimming research + harness prototypes, 27.09.2026) - sources added and how they are applied ====
Pass done BEFORE the prototypes; base = C:/dev/occt8-mig/lit-offset/LITERATURE.txt (20 sources, P1..P6 ranking).
Added (DOIs checked by web search 27.09):
B1. Rossignac J.R., Requicha A.A.G. Offsetting operations in solid modelling. Computer Aided Geometric Design
    3(2):129-148, 1986. doi:10.1016/0167-8396(86)90017-8. Venue: CAGD (specialist). [ABS]
    Idea: the offset of a solid is its Minkowski sum with a ball = the solid united with the swept faces, edge
    cylinders and vertex balls; constant-radius rounds/fillets are offset-of-offset (opening/closing).
    Applied: (i) E4/E5-union = union of slabs + edge tubes + vertex balls; (ii) the exactness condition of E1
    (removing blends then offsetting): exact only when the blend is a closing/opening of the sharp solid, i.e. a
    closed blend network; own derivation from the ball semigroup property: dilate_d(close_r X) = dilate_d X for
    d >= r (dilate_r X is r-open). Open-ended fillets (fillet ends on convex faces) are NOT closings -> E1 wrong
    there (measured, runs/eval-db.txt).
B2. Zhu H., Menq C.-H. B-rep model simplification by automatic fillet/round suppressing for efficient automatic
    feature recognition. Computer-Aided Design 34(2):109-123, 2002. doi:10.1016/S0010-4485(01)00056-2. Venue: CAD. [ABS]
    Idea: recognise blend faces (chains, disks) and suppress them by extending the neighbours.
    Applied: E1 uses OCCT's existing BRepAlgoAPI_Defeaturing (the same operation) on the folding faces.
B3. Jacobson A., Kavan L., Sorkine-Hornung O. Robust inside-outside segmentation using generalized winding numbers.
    ACM TOG 32(4), 2013. doi:10.1145/2461912.2461916. Venue: TOG (IF ~7). [ABS]
    Idea: the winding number classifies inside/outside for self-intersecting (and open) surfaces.
    Applied: E5 classifies every split face by signed ray crossings (integer winding numbers of closed element sets).
B4. Zhou Q., Grinspun E., Zorin D., Jacobson A. Mesh arrangements for solid geometry. ACM TOG 35(4), 2016.
    doi:10.1145/2897824.2925901. Venue: TOG (IF ~7). [ABS + author page]
    Idea: resolve all intersections (arrangement), propagate winding numbers across patches, extract
    {w >= 1} = regularized self-union of a self-intersecting input.
    Applied: E2/E3 (MakerVolume cells + propagation) and E5 (General Fuse split faces + ray winding): the raw
    offset shell of the stock result, or the union of elements, is resolved by the arrangement and its w >= 1
    region kept. This replaces "decide what to trim by distance" (Autodesk EP4530910A1 design-around, IP.txt).
Re-used from the base file: Seong-Elber-Kim 2006 (distance criterion - here ONLY the test oracle and the
stage-A control), Farouki 1986 / Maekawa 1999 (fold factor 1 - d*k), Lee 2009 (union construction = P6),
Hong 2019 / Park 2021 (the ends of the self-intersection curve are numerically unstable - observed here as the
sliver faces that break E5 on stepn and the tolerance blow-up on 5829/031).
Better-known method check: for exact B-rep trimming the literature has no method cheaper than tracing the
self-intersection curves (Seong-Elber-Kim; Park 2021 BVH + miter handling); arrangement + winding is the general
robust method for meshes (B3, B4). Neither is available in OCCT 8.0.1 in a robust form: the General Fuse fails or
blows tolerances up on raw offsets with cusps/tangent folds (stage B evidence).

==== Stage A2 (structural guard of thick solids / offsets, 27.09.2026) - sources added and how they are applied ====
Pass done BEFORE the code (DOIs/venues checked by web search 27.09). Question: what is the cheapest complete
set of TOPOLOGICAL conditions that every valid shelled/offset solid satisfies, so that a result breaking one of
them is provably invalid (error), without always paying a full BRepCheck/BOP check?
A2-1. Requicha A.A.G. Representations for rigid solids: theory, methods, and systems. ACM Computing Surveys
     12(4):437-464, 1980. doi:10.1145/356827.356833. Venue: ACM CSUR (IF ~16). [full text, open PDF]
     Idea: an r-set's boundary representation is valid only if its boundary is a closed, orientable 2-manifold;
     combinatorially: every edge bounds exactly two faces of a shell and is traversed by them in opposite
     directions (the "Moebius rule").
     Applied: solidShellsBroken034 = the edge-use test per shell of every solid of the result (edge used once
     -> open; used by two faces in the same direction -> incoherent). It is exactly the manifold part of
     BRepCheck_Shell::Closed/Orientation, so only results BRepCheck rejects can change; O(E) with one hash map.
A2-2. Forsyth M. Shelling and offsetting bodies. Proc. 3rd ACM Symposium on Solid Modeling and Applications
     (SMA '95 = SPM series), 373-381, 1995. doi:10.1145/218013.218088. Venue: SPM (specialist). [ABS]
     Idea: shelling = offset of the kept faces + the rim that joins each opening to its offset; the topology of
     the shelled body follows from the kept faces and the openings.
     Applied (own derivation from this construction): each connected piece of the kept faces that has a free
     boundary (an opening) is joined to its offset into ONE shell; a closed piece (e.g. the wall of an inner
     void) keeps its shell and gets a second one -> the thick solid has at most sum(1 or 2) shells
     (maxThickShells034, union-find over faces, O(E)). The owner-relevant failure (box filleted on all 12
     edges, top removed: the opening is not cut, the offset shell stays closed and apart) breaks this bound.
A2-3. Gu H., Chase T.R., Cheney D.C., Bailey T., Johnson D. Identifying, correcting, and avoiding errors in
     computer-aided design models which affect interoperability. J. Computing and Information Science in
     Engineering 1(2):156-166, 2001. doi:10.1115/1.1384887. Venue: JCISE (specialist). [ABS]
     Idea: classification of B-rep defects (free edges/gaps, inconsistent face orientation, extra or
     unconnected shells, self-intersections) and detecting them by cheap topological tests before costly
     geometric ones.
     Applied: the order of the guard - O(E) topology first (always on), geometric checks (BRepCheck + BOP
     self-interference) only when a cheap condition says the geometry is at risk.
A2-4. Bischoff S., Kobbelt L. Structure preserving CAD model repair. Computer Graphics Forum 24(3):527-536, 2005
     (Eurographics 2005). Venue: CGF (specialist). [author PDF; DOI not returned by the search]
     Idea: CAD defects concentrate where adjacent patches meet tangentially or nearly so (gaps, overlaps that
     topology alone cannot see).
     Applied: the second trigger of the geometric check - a TANGENT edge (BRepOffset_Analyse: ChFiDS_Tangential)
     of a FREE-FORM face under the INTERSECTION join, where the two offset surfaces are tangent and their
     intersection is ill-conditioned (measured, runs/tanint-*.txt: 4 of 6 NURBS cases came back BRepCheck-valid
     but self-intersecting, e.g. NURBS box r5 on 8 edges thick +1 -> volume 7140 instead of 18119 of the analytic
     twin). Analytic faces are excluded: OCCT resolves their tangency exactly (analytic twins all correct).
     REJECTED variant (measured, runs/time-corp.txt of the intermediate build): the same trigger for the ARC join
     when a tangent free-form face contracts (factor < 0.75) caught M5 (pocketn +0.5) but made ordinary valid
     NURBS fillet offsets 35 -> 1673 ms and 47 -> 4488 ms (bfiln -1): a noticeable slowdown of common offsets, so
     M5 stays for stage B2 (free-form local self-intersection class).
Re-used: Farouki 1986 / Maekawa 1999 (stage A fold trigger, unchanged), Seong-Elber-Kim 2006 (distance control of
stage A, unchanged; A2 adds no distance test).
Better-known method check: a full BRepCheck of every result (Mantyla-style validity) is the complete method but
doubles the offset time (stage A measurement); the Euler-Poincare formula V-E+F-(L-F)=2(S-G) was considered and
NOT used - it is always satisfiable by some genus G for a combinatorially closed shell and adds no power beyond
the edge-use test. Certified intersection tests (stage A upgrade path) remain the stronger/slower option.

==== Lane A3 (CLASS: thick solids whose removed faces are G1-tangent to their neighbours, 27.09.2026) ====
Pass done BEFORE the code (web search 27.09; venues checked; Parasolid quarantine not opened). Question: what is the
rim of an opening when the removed face meets the kept faces tangentially, and why does OCCT's rule (extend the
offset of every neighbour to the removed face and trim it there) fail for exactly that condition?
A3-1. Forsyth M. Shelling and offsetting bodies. Proc. 3rd ACM Symp. Solid Modeling and Applications (SPM series),
     373-381, 1995. doi:10.1145/218013.218088. Venue: SPM (specialist). [ABS] (re-used from A2)
     Idea: a shelled body = kept faces + their offsets + rim faces that join each opening edge to its offset.
     Applied: the rim is built per opening EDGE (not per removed face), so one removed face can have edges of two
     kinds; round 1 implements removed faces whose edges are all tangent, the mixed kind is detected (next round).
A3-2. Rossignac J.R., Requicha A.A.G. Offsetting operations in solid modelling. CAGD 3(2):129-148, 1986.
     doi:10.1016/0167-8396(86)90017-8. Venue: CAGD (specialist). [ABS] (re-used from stage B)
     Idea: offset = union of face slabs, edge tubes and vertex balls; along a G1 edge the two face slabs share the
     offset edge and no tube exists.
     Applied (own derivation): along a G1 edge E between a kept face F and a removed face C (offset 0), the offset
     of F at E is E + t*n where n is ALSO the normal of C; so offset(F) is parallel to C at distance |t| near E and
     does not cross the surface of C there. The rim along E is the ruled strip {P + s*t*n(P), P in E, s in [0,1]}
     (the slab side of F at a boundary edge). A vertex of such an edge whose faces are all G1 there has ONE offset
     point, even when it lies on the opening (free edges carry no normal).
A3-3. Ye X., Maekawa T. Differential geometry of intersection curves of two surfaces. CAGD 16(8):767-788, 1999.
     doi:10.1016/S0167-8396(99)00018-7. Venue: CAGD (specialist). [ABS]
     Idea: at a tangential contact of two surfaces the intersection is singular (parallel normals; the curve is not
     given by first-order data); transversal and tangential intersections need different treatment.
     Applied: explains the stock failure: ToContext/ExtentFace intersects offset(F) with C; at a tangent edge the
     two surfaces are parallel at |t| (inward: no intersection at all - the OCCT source itself notes it in
     BRepOffset_Tool::ExtentFace; outward: an unrelated far crossing). Hence tangent edges must not go through the
     context intersection at all - a classification, not a tolerance tweak.
A3-4. Lee S.H. Offsetting operations on non-manifold topological models. Computer-Aided Design 41(11):830-846, 2009
     (ScienceDirect pii S0010448509001523). Venue: CAD (specialist). [ABS]
     Idea: thickening of a sheet = offset faces + side faces at the free boundary edges, the side face generated by
     the boundary edge and its offset along the normal; shelling and thickening share one construction.
     Applied: along tangent opening edges the kept faces are treated as a sheet with a free boundary and OCCT's own
     thickening wall construction (BRepOffset_MakeOffset::MakeMissingWalls, LGPL) builds the rim; the removed face
     is dropped (it has no ring: the ring width F_t-to-C is zero along a tangent edge).
A3-5. Ravi Kumar G.V.V. et al. Computing constant offsets of a NURBS B-Rep. Computer-Aided Design 35(10), 2003
     (pii S0010448502002105). Venue: CAD. [ABS]
     Idea: offset each face, then remove gaps/overlaps between offset faces, then sew; faces meeting G1 need no gap
     filling because their offsets share the offset edge.
     Applied: the same sharing rule for the offset VERTEX of a tangent fan on the opening (tangentFan034): the stock
     code shared it only when every edge at the vertex was tangent, so vertices on the opening got one copy per
     face (open wires, BRepCheck NotClosed) although the geometry was right.
Better-known method check: a general shelling by Booleans (Yan, Zhang, Yong, Peng, Sun, "Shelling algorithm in
solid modeling", CIS 2004, LNCS 3314, doi:10.1007/978-3-540-30497-5_46 - conference, not IF>=5; [ABS]) = offset
solid + regularized Boolean; it needs a correct closed offset and a robust Boolean with thin walls, and it does not
define the rim either. The edge-wise rim classification above is cheaper (no Boolean) and exact.

==== Stage A2 round 2 (legal extra shells = cavities of the hollow, 27.09.2026) - sources added and how they are applied ====
Pass done BEFORE the code (venues/DOIs checked by web search 27.09). Question from review A2 r1: when is a thick solid
with MORE shells than the pieces of the kept faces correct (the offset pinches off at a neck thinner than 2|t|), and
what topological condition separates it from the broken "opening not cut" result, without a full BRepCheck/BOP run?
A2R2-1. Rossignac J.R., Requicha A.A.G. Offsetting operations in solid modelling. Computer Aided Geometric Design
     3(2):129-148, 1986. doi:10.1016/0167-8396(86)90017-8. Venue: CAGD (specialist, full source). [ABS + citations]
     Idea: offsetting is defined on the point set (S grown/shrunk by r, regularized); a shell (hollow) is the set
     difference of the solid and its inner offset; the offset operators do NOT preserve topology - the inner offset
     can split into several components (and components can vanish).
     Applied: a thick solid's boundary = kept faces + offset of the kept faces + walls on the removed faces. A
     component of the hollow that no opening reaches is a closed CAVITY bounded by offset faces only (neck thinner
     than 2|t| inward; outward: a slot narrower than 2t sealed around a chamber). So extra shells are legal exactly
     when they are such cavities (thickShells034).
A2R2-2. Lee S.H. Offsetting operations on non-manifold topological models. Computer-Aided Design 41(11):830-846,
     2009 (ScienceDirect PII S0010448509001523; the DOI was not returned by the search). Venue: CAD (specialist, full
     source). [ABS]
     Idea: the offset model is built from offset elements generated by faces, edges and vertices of the input,
     and the topology of the result (shells, cavities) is derived from which input element generated each part.
     Applied: every face of the result is classified by its GENERATOR, read from the offset's own history
     (myInitOffsetFace roots: kept face / edge / vertex -> offset; removed face -> wall; glued input face -> kept),
     never from geometry. A shell is ANCHORED (holds kept faces) or a CAVITY (offset faces only).
A2R2-3. Mantyla M. An Introduction to Solid Modeling. Computer Science Press, 1988, ISBN 0-88175-108-1. [book]
     Idea: a solid's boundary = one outer shell + one inner shell per cavity; shells are independent components of
     the boundary (Euler operators add a shell per void).
     Applied: counting shells alone (A2 r1 bound "1 per open piece, 2 per closed piece") cannot see cavities, which
     are a legal extra shell each; the count stays only as the cheap gate for the proof below.
Re-used: Requicha 1980 (A2-1, closed coherent shells: check (1) unchanged), Forsyth 1995 (A2-2, the wall of an
opening joins the kept piece to its offset), Gu et al. 2001 (A2-3, topology first, geometry only when needed).
Own derivation (not in the sources): the wall of an opening is the band of the removed face between its rim (edges
shared with kept faces) and the offset, so every piece of it touches the rim -> lies in an anchored shell and meets
an offset face. Therefore (i) a shell without kept faces that holds a wall is impossible (the offset shell was closed
across the opening) -> error, always, O(F+E); (ii) more shells than pieces are accepted without any check when every
shell without kept faces is made of known offset faces only and every wall meets an offset face; (iii) otherwise
(faces whose generator the history does not know, e.g. rebuilt after the history was filled, or a wall that meets no
offset face: an opening closed at the rim) the construction does not explain the extra shell -> the reviewer's safety
net: BRepCheck + BOP self-interference, error only if it fails.
Better-known method check: a full BRepCheck + BOP self-interference check of every thick solid is the complete but
slow method (review r1 fallback: correct, but pays the geometric check on every legal cavity); a point-classification
test that the cavity lies inside the outer shell (BRepClass3d) was considered and NOT used - it is geometric, the
generator argument above already fixes where a cavity can come from, and no member of the class needed it.
