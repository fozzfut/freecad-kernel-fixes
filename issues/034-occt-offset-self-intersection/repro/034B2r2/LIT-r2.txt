Issue 034 lane B2, round 2 (28.09.2026) - literature and provenance of the round-2 design elements.
(Venue rule: specialist CAD/CAGD journals count as full sources. OA = open access read; ABS = abstract/known result only.)

A. Proven vanishing decision (review finding 4)
 1. T. Maekawa, "An overview of offset curves and surfaces", Computer-Aided Design 31(3) 1999, 165-173,
    doi 10.1016/S0010-4485(99)00013-5 (CAD). Offset factors 1 - d*k_i; local self-intersection iff a factor <= 0.
    APPLIED: the vanishing condition (every factor pair has a non-positive member) and the fold/cusp vocabulary.
 2. T. Maekawa, N.M. Patrikalakis et al., "Self-intersections of offsets of polynomial / rational surfaces"
    (Maekawa, Cho, Patrikalakis, IJSM 1999 / CAGD line of work; interval projected polyhedron). ABS.
    APPLIED: the idea that offset-singularity questions are decided robustly by Bernstein-form interval bounds with
    subdivision (our test is a bound, not a root finder).
 3. T.W. Sederberg, R.J. Meyers, "Loop detection in surface patch intersections", CAGD 5(2) 1988 161-171 (CAGD).
    ABS. APPLIED: convex hull of hodograph nets bounds every derivative over a Bezier patch.
 4. C.-Y. Hu, T. Maekawa, N.M. Patrikalakis, X. Ye, "Robust interval algorithm for surface intersections",
    CAD 29(9) 1997 617-627 (CAD). ABS. APPLIED: interval arithmetic on Bezier data with subdivision; rounding margin.
 5. Lane R-034g (own, 27.09): trace/determinant conditions C1 = 2 s|N|^3 - dJ, C2 = s^2|N|^4 - s d|N|J + d^2 Q for
    "both eigenvalues of s I - d W positive" (reviewed as correct in Review R-034g r1). APPLIED in the opposite
    direction: a patch is proven vanishing when the upper bound of C1 or of C2 is negative. Own derivation of the
    Leibniz interval form for rational patches (S_s = (P_s - w_s S)/w, S_ss, S_st, S_tt) and of the rotation step
    (the factors are invariant under rigid motions; bounds are taken in the patch's own chord frame).
 6. OCCT GeomConvert_SurfToAnaSurf (canonical recognition within a tolerance; OCCT's own conversion tool) + the
    analytic offset rule of BRepOffset::Surface (radius + d within Precision::Confusion -> Degenerated). APPLIED to
    the degenerate case d = r only, where no interval bound can prove a factor of exactly 0 (tolerance decision,
    the same one the analytic twin gets).

B. Cap and rim sections (review finding 2) - own derivation on top of round 1's rim lemma
 7. Rim lemma (round 1, LIT-r1c (4)): two rim offsets of a vanishing set meet inside their own domains
    (inside for d > r, at the ends for d = r). Consequence used now: the stock section of a rim face on a removed
    face (the offset of its edge, extended to the removed face) already carries the corner with the other rim faces;
    its OTHER end is the stock end at a non-vanishing neighbour (tube, surviving blend, second removed face) and is
    kept (the round-1 replacement by a whole enlarged-copy section lost that end: L housings, mixed radii).
 8. Own derivation: a removed face is not offset, so the corner of two rim offsets with a removed face that the set
    reaches lies BEYOND the stock end (distance <= size of the set + |d|): the stock sections (rim x removed face,
    rim x rim across a sharp edge) are prolonged by that reach at their vanishing ends only (two removed faces,
    removed face tangent to the set).
 9. M. Forsyth, "Shelling and offsetting bodies", ACM Solid Modeling (SMA) 1995, doi 10.1145/218013.218088 (as cited in round 1) and
    J.R. Rossignac, A.A.G. Requicha, "Offsetting operations in solid modelling", CAGD 3 1986 129-148 (CAGD):
    faces may vanish and the surviving neighbours are extended and intersected (kept from round 1). The references
    of the test stand (erosion/dilation by primitives + Booleans) follow Rossignac-Requicha.

C. Partial folds (review finding 1) - studied, NOT implemented in round 2
 10. J. Park, Q Youn Hong, M.-S. Kim, G. Elber, "Self-intersection computation for freeform surfaces based on a
     regional representation scheme for miter points", CAGD 2021 (S0167839621000248) (CAGD). ABS.
 11. "Fast Determination and Computation of Self-intersections for NURBS Surfaces", ACM TOG 2025,
     doi 10.1145/3727620 (TOG/SIGGRAPH). ABS: osculating toroidal patches bound the surface; miter points detected.
 12. A. Pekerman, G. Elber, M.-S. Kim, "Self-intersection detection and elimination in freeform curves and
     surfaces", CAD 40(2) 2008 150-159 (CAD). ABS.
 13. J.-K. Seong, G. Elber, M.-S. Kim, "Trimming local and global self-intersections in offset curves/surfaces
     using distance maps", CAD 38 2006 183-193 (CAD). ABS. NOT applied (distance-map region finding is the
     EP4530910A1 red flag; only curvature/fold-curve based trimming is allowed, IP.txt).
 14. Q Youn Hong et al., C&G 2019 (support). ABS.
     Design recorded for round 3 (not code): split the face along its traced fold curve (predictor-corrector on
     1 - d k = 0), prove the negative part vanishing with a regional (miter-point) certificate - the interval test of
     A cannot certify a region whose boundary has factor exactly 0 -, and join the positive parts through the rim
     machinery; swallowtail folds (cusp edges, the owner cube Face26, 5829 corner lobes) additionally need the
     self-intersection curve with miter end points (10, 11).

D. Patent position (EP4530910A1 design-around, IP.txt of issue 034): round 2 adds no distance evaluation to the
   kernel; decisions use curvature bounds (A), the analytic rule (A6) and topology/section geometry (B). The stand's
   distance oracle stays a test-side, error-raising control only.
